A biogas plant is an industry

And it should be run like one: with procedures, clear responsibilities and numbers checked every day. Here is how we work.

The starting point

The cost that matters is the cost of methane

Many plants buy feedstock by the tonne. But a digester does not produce tonnes: it produces methane. A feedstock that looks cheap can yield very little, and the methane it produces ends up costing much more.

That is why we measure everything on one indicator: what it costs to produce a cubic metre of methane. It shows which recipe pays off, which contracts to revisit and where the plant is losing margin.

Results do not depend on the machines. They depend on how feedstock, biology, field work and data are managed.

Recipe, procurement and process have to be read together: changing only one of the three is rarely enough.

Case study · Full management of a biogas plant

From cost per tonne to cost per methane

We ran an agricultural biogas plant with our own operators on site. The recipe was bought in €/tonne: some feedstocks, cheap on paper, had very low yields, and at the same price their methane cost up to ten times more, with biological instability and more digestate to spread.

What we did

  1. New KPI: cost per Nm³ of methane, tracked every day.
  2. Recipe redesigned with high-energy feedstock and the nitrogen balance under control.
  3. Feedstock yard brought in-house: operators working to standard procedures, direct purchasing, every load traced in an app. This is where Methan.IQ was born.
  4. Preventive maintenance: quarterly plan, critical spare parts in stock, emergency procedures.
Result

The new recipe reduces feedstock cost from about €680,000 to about €360,000 on an annual basis (about €320,000 less), with an average methane cost of around €0.15/Nm³.
