Building the supply chain before authorisation: what to document

18 November 2026

Biomass supply must be structured and documented before authorisation: suppliers, feedstock mix, quality of deliveries and traceability.

Every biogas or biomethane plant begins with a practical question: what will we feed it with, and for how long? We think the answer comes too often once the project is already well advanced. This is our view: the availability of biomass cannot be taken for granted; it must be built and documented.

Let's take a hypothetical example. A developer declares biomass availability based on discussions with local farms. It seems like a good starting point. But how much of that biomass is backed by a written commitment? What is its quality? For how long? Without answers, availability remains merely an intention.

For this reason, in our view, it is worth putting four elements in order before authorisation. Qualified suppliers: who they are, what they can supply, and with what continuity. The feedstock mix: the blend of materials the plant plans to feed into the digester, with the quantities, within the digester's biological limits. The quality of deliveries: how it will be verified on receipt and by what method. Traceability: where each load originates, how it travels, and how it is recorded.

This work serves two purposes. It makes the project readable to those who must assess it, because it shows where the plant's feed will come from. And it makes the plant manageable once it is running, because the planned feedstock mix and the one actually loaded can be compared using data rather than memory. Neither requires promises of yield: both require documents.

The biomass market is local and must be mapped catchment by catchment. FAR.E. is developing Feedstock Exchange.
